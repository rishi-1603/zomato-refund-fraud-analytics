# Refund Exposure Concentration (Pareto Analysis)
### How concentrated is refund value among customers?

> Generated by `scripts/pareto_analysis.py` — every figure computed live from
> `data/processed/zomato_with_refunds.csv` (real Kaggle order data + disclosed
> seeded synthetic refund layer). Pinned by `tests/test_pareto_analysis.py`.

## Headline result

- **9,895 customers** appear in the data; 2,515 have ≥1 refund, 7,380 have none
- Total refund exposure: **₹974,344** (historical refund VALUE,
  not confirmed fraud loss)

| Customer cut | Customers | Share of refund value |
|---|---|---|
| Top 1% | 99 | **26.9%** |
| Top 5% | 495 | **60.6%** |
| Top 10% | 990 | **75.6%** |
| Top 20% | 1,979 | **95.1%** |

| Value covered | Customers needed | % of all customers |
|---|---|---|

| 80% of refund value | 1,165 | 11.8% |

## Cross-check with the risk pipeline

The 279 flagged suspects (2.8% of customers) hold **38.78%** of total refund exposure — matching `reports/score_validation.md` exactly. The risk pipeline and this analysis agree on the same underlying data.

## Business interpretation

1. **Refund exposure is heavily concentrated.** The top 75.6% of customers hold 10% → review effort scales sub-linearly: a small, well-chosen review queue covers a disproportionate share of exposure.
2. **The 50% line sits at 3.0% of customers** — reviewing fewer than 300 accounts would cover half of all refund value in this dataset.
3. **Concentration ≠ guilt.** High refund value can have legitimate
   causes (high order volume, genuinely bad delivery experiences). Phases 2–4 of this program (baseline normalization, sensitivity, false-positive analysis) test exactly that before anyone acts on a flag.
4. **Method caveat:** the refund layer is synthetic and seeded; these
   cuts are reproducible demonstrations of the method, not measured    business losses.
